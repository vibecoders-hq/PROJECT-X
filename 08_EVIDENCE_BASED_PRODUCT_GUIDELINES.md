# 08 — EVIDENCE-BASED PRODUCT GUIDELINES
## PROJECT-X | Cognitive Wellbeing, Reminiscence, UX & Safety

**Purpose:** Translate current evidence into practical product and implementation rules for PROJECT-X.  
**Important:** This is product guidance, not a medical protocol.

---

## 1. What PROJECT-X Is Actually Solving

PROJECT-X must NOT be treated as simply a “brain-game website.”

Dementia can affect memory, thinking, communication, mood, behaviour and everyday functioning. It can also lead to withdrawal from social activities, anxiety, loss of confidence, increasing dependence and significant caregiver burden.

There is no cure for dementia. However, evidence supports non-pharmacological approaches that can improve quality of life and daily functioning, including cognitive stimulation, physical activity, social engagement and caregiver support.

### Product goal

PROJECT-X should help seniors:
- stay mentally engaged
- participate in meaningful activities
- reconnect with familiar memories
- stay socially connected
- manage simple daily routines
- maintain dignity and independence

It should also help caregivers see useful engagement patterns.

**Never claim that PROJECT-X cures, reverses, diagnoses, or medically measures dementia.**

---

## 2. Why Generic Brain Games Are Not Enough

A generic task such as:

> “Remember these 6 random objects.”

does not create a strong connection with the user's life.

PROJECT-X should combine:

**Cognitive activity + familiarity + emotion + conversation + culture**

Instead of an abstract memory test, a familiar tea-garden scene could prompt recognition, recall or conversation.

The senior should feel that they are **remembering something meaningful**, not taking an exam.

---

## 3. Reminiscence-Centered Experience

Personalized activities and reminiscence-oriented approaches are relevant to dementia-care guidance.

Potential content:
- familiar places
- household objects
- clothing and textiles
- food and cooking memories
- music and instruments
- festivals
- family life
- childhood memories
- village/town environments
- occupations and everyday routines
- nature and landscapes

### Critical rule

**Culture must not be decoration.**

Do not add a North-East pattern to a generic game and call it culturally relevant.

Use cultural material as the actual content that makes the activity meaningful.

---

## 4. North-Eastern Region Direction

NER-specific cultural content should be treated as a product hypothesis to validate with real users and local experts.

Possible categories:
- tea gardens
- markets
- rivers and hills
- traditional homes
- clothing and textiles
- food
- festivals
- folk music and instruments
- agricultural/rural scenes
- traditional objects
- regional stories and languages

Do not treat North-East India as one homogeneous culture.

Where possible, personalize using:
- state/region
- preferred language
- cultural background
- personal memories
- interests

**Personalization is more important than stereotypes.**

---

## 5. Cognitive Experience Model

The MVP should contain a small number of meaningful activities rather than dozens of shallow games.

### Memory
- image recall
- object matching
- familiar-place recognition
- sequence recall

### Attention
- find a target
- identify changes
- simple visual/audio attention tasks

### Recognition
- familiar objects
- places
- sounds
- patterns

### Daily Routine Recall
- morning routine
- medicine/hydration reminders
- appointments
- simple activity planning

### Reminiscence
- familiar images
- music/sounds
- prompts about past experiences
- optional voice responses

---

## 6. AI Should Adapt the Experience — Not Diagnose

AI can observe interaction signals such as:
- completion
- accuracy
- response time
- repeated mistakes
- abandonment
- preferred content
- session duration
- difficulty tolerance

Then adapt:
- difficulty
- number of items
- complexity
- repetition
- pacing
- content familiarity

### Example

If a user performs well with familiar image recognition but struggles with long sequences:

**Next session:**
- keep familiar imagery
- reduce sequence length
- gradually increase difficulty
- provide encouragement
- avoid making the user feel tested

### Never do this

Do NOT output:

> “Your dementia has progressed 12%.”

Do NOT diagnose:

> “You may have Alzheimer's.”

Use non-diagnostic language:
- Activity progress
- Engagement trend
- Recent performance
- Practice history
- Caregiver observation

---

## 7. Emotional Design

Avoid:
- intimidating test screens
- harsh failure states
- stressful countdowns
- complex dashboards
- medical-looking interfaces
- excessive text
- confusing navigation

Prefer:
- encouragement
- familiar imagery
- calm motion
- large touch targets
- simple choices
- positive feedback
- optional repetition
- gentle audio
- predictable navigation

**Target feeling:** “I am enjoying remembering,” not “I am being tested.”

---

## 8. Voice-First Interaction

Voice can reduce navigation complexity.

Example:

> “Namaskar, Kamala. What would you like to do today?”

Possible choices:
- Play a memory game
- Listen to music
- See today's activities
- Talk to my family

Voice should be:
- optional
- slow and clear
- supported by visible controls
- tolerant of mistakes
- available in preferred languages where feasible

Voice must never be the only way to use the application.

---

## 9. Caregiver Experience

PROJECT-X has two important users.

### Senior needs
- simple interaction
- meaningful activity
- encouragement
- familiarity
- dignity

### Caregiver needs
- simple visibility
- reminders
- recent activity
- engagement trends
- appropriate alerts
- understandable summaries

Example:

**Today's activity**
- Memory activity: Completed
- Attention activity: Completed
- Cultural activity: Completed
- Session duration: 8 min
- Reminder status: 3/4 completed
- Recent engagement: Stable

Avoid overwhelming caregivers with unnecessary analytics.

---

## 10. Social Connection

Eventually support:
- family memory prompts
- shared music
- “Do you remember this?” cards
- family-submitted photographs
- conversation prompts
- shared activities

The product should move beyond:

**Senior ↔ App**

toward:

**Senior ↔ App ↔ Family/Caregiver**

The application supports human connection; it does not replace it.

---

## 11. Physical Activity

Physical activity is relevant to healthy ageing and dementia care, but it should NOT become the core MVP.

Future possibilities:
- gentle movement prompts
- rhythm activities
- stretching reminders
- short walking prompts

Do not provide unsafe exercise instructions or imply that PROJECT-X provides medical rehabilitation.

---

## 12. Offline-First Requirement

### Should work offline
- core games
- user preferences
- local progress
- basic reminders
- cached cultural content
- voice assets where practical

### When connectivity returns
- synchronize progress
- update caregiver information
- retrieve new content
- perform non-critical cloud operations

The core senior experience must not depend on continuous internet access.

---

## 13. Accessibility Rules

Prioritize:
- large text
- high contrast
- large touch targets
- simple layouts
- minimal simultaneous choices
- consistent navigation
- clear icons + text
- audio assistance
- no critical information conveyed only by colour
- forgiving interaction
- obvious Back/Home controls

Animations should feel calming and alive, not distracting.

---

## 14. Landing Page / First Impression

The landing page should create an emotional hook immediately.

Desired feeling:

**warm + nostalgic + beautiful + culturally rooted + trustworthy**

Possible opening:

> **“Some memories deserve to be remembered.”**

Then show a calm, visually rich North-East environment and invite:

> **“What would you like to remember today?”**

Primary action:

**Play**

Secondary action:

**Talk to PROJECT-X / How it works**

The landing page should feel like a welcoming **memory garden**, not a government portal or hospital dashboard.

```text
Senior Profile

      ↓
Interaction
      ↓
Performance Signals

AI Adaptation
      ↓
```

> **AI continuously adapts meaningful, culturally familiar cognitive experiences to the individual while keeping caregivers informed.**

## 17. Claims We Can Safely Make

Prefer:
- “Designed to support cognitive engagement and wellbeing.”
- “Personalized cognitive activities.”
- “Culturally familiar experiences.”
- “Helps caregivers monitor engagement.”
- “Designed for low-connectivity environments.”

Avoid:
- “Cures dementia.”
- “Prevents Alzheimer's.”
- “Diagnoses dementia.”
- “Measures dementia progression.”
- “Reverses memory loss.”
- “Clinically proven” unless we actually have clinical evidence.

---

## 19. Instructions for Coding Agents

Before implementing a feature, ask:

1. Does this genuinely help the senior?
2. Does it reduce cognitive/interaction burden?
3. Does it support meaningful engagement?
4. Can the senior understand it without technical knowledge?
5. Does it preserve dignity?
6. Does it work with low connectivity where required?
7. Is the AI doing something meaningful?
8. Are we accidentally presenting entertainment data as medical diagnosis?
9. Does it support the caregiver without replacing the caregiver?
10. Does it make the product more memorable to a hackathon judge?

### Final principle

**Do not build a brain-game website.**

Build a:

> **Culturally familiar cognitive-wellbeing companion that helps seniors engage, remember, connect and maintain daily routines — while giving caregivers useful, non-diagnostic insight.**
